# Tall infographics and carousels

Distinguish a tall page from a carousel or presentation. A tall infographic has one continuous reading path; a carousel has separate pages and should remain understandable at each stopping point. Do not silently switch between these outputs. Native tall dimensions are bounded to 4000 pixels per side; use crops to inspect text at real viewing size. Content beyond that limit needs an explicitly agreed split, not smaller and smaller type.
For a tall graphic plan header, meaningful section hierarchy, rhythm, transitions, conclusion and sources. Avoid seams that look like unrelated posters. For a carousel establish the hook, purposeful progression and closing action. Do not manufacture suspense or hide essential qualifications on a later page. Repeat just enough orientation for a viewer joining midway.
Use create_canvas for one tall page or create_document format tall_infographic for linked sections; create_document format carousel supports 1–40 same-sized pages. All pages in one export share dimensions. Keep the same type scale and palette across pages. Preserve source notes and exact figures even when simplifying visible copy. Restructure or split dense content before shrinking it.
Review every page at target phone width, then check progression as a storyboard. Test critical labels with inspect_canvas or inspect_document detail crops. Ensure the final call to action is meaningful for the audience, not generic filler. Keep all headings, numbers and labels as native text objects. Read infographic-frameworks, art-direction and quality-review.
