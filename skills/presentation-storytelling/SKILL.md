# Presentation storytelling

Begin with the audience's decision or learning objective and the available speaking time. Reuse supplied answers; ask only for missing information that changes the story. Distinguish what exists today from proposals, forecasts and unknowns. Do not invent traction or evidence to fill a pitch pattern.
Choose a narrative appropriate to the purpose. A proposal can move from situation and stakes to options, evidence and recommendation. A lesson can establish a mental model, demonstrate it, offer an example and check understanding. A research talk can frame the question, method, findings and limitations. A pitch can show problem, answer, workflow, proof and ask; this is an option, not a compulsory template.
Write a storyboard before composition: page ID, title, single message, evidence source, visual explanation, transition, section and speaker notes. Claim-style titles are useful when evidence supports them; do not overstate conclusions. Put spoken detail in notes rather than shrinking body text. If timing is estimated from word count, label it an estimate and rehearse the actual delivery.
Use create_document for a flexible 1–40-page native presentation. All pages share a canvas size and theme; elements and notes remain independently editable. Use legacy create_deck only for the existing fixed five-slide workflow. Compose pages in bounded edits, inspect individual pages and the storyboard, then export_document and send_document_to_canva. Import success needs separate Canva layer/fidelity review. Read art-direction and quality-review. Do not generate an image of an entire slide as the final editable deliverable.
